# Market Positioning Methodology

## What is Positioning?

**Positioning is the battle for the mind.**

It's not what you do to your product. It's what you do to the mind of your prospect.

**Your positioning determines:**
- Who considers you
- What they think you do
- Why they choose you (or don't)
- How much they'll pay
- Who you compete against

---

## The Positioning Statement Formula

### The Core Template

```
For [TARGET CUSTOMER]
Who [HAVE THIS PROBLEM/NEED]
[PRODUCT NAME] is a [CATEGORY]
That [KEY BENEFIT/SOLUTION]
Unlike [COMPETITION]
We [UNIQUE DIFFERENTIATOR]
```

### Example (Uber, early days)

```
For urban professionals
Who need reliable transportation without the hassle
Uber is a ride-hailing app
That gets you a car in minutes with one tap
Unlike traditional taxis
We provide upfront pricing, cashless payment, and driver ratings
```

## The 5 Whys (Unpacking Positioning)

When helping CEO understand positioning, use Socratic questioning:

### Question 1: WHO is this for? (Target Customer)

**Ask:**
- Who feels the pain most acutely?
- Who is willing to pay for a solution?
- Who can you reach efficiently?
- Who will become evangelists?

**Red Flag:** "Everyone" or "businesses" (too broad)
**Good Answer:** "Marketing directors at B2B SaaS companies with 10-50 employees"

---

### Question 2: WHAT problem do they have? (Pain Point)

**Ask:**
- What keeps them up at night?
- What's the cost of the current solution?
- What have they tried that failed?
- How do they describe this pain in their own words?

**Red Flag:** Problem you invented (not real pain)
**Good Answer:** Specific, visceral pain they already feel

---

### Question 3: WHY your solution? (Unique Value)

**Ask:**
- What makes you different from alternatives?
- What can you do that others can't/won't?
- What's your unfair advantage?
- Why would someone choose you over the incumbent?

**Red Flag:** "We're better" or "We have AI" (generic)
**Good Answer:** Specific capability or approach that's defensible

---

### Question 4: WHY should they believe you? (Proof)

**Ask:**
- What evidence supports your claims?
- Who else has succeeded with this?
- What results can you guarantee?
- What's your track record?

**Red Flag:** "Trust us" or "Revolutionary" (no proof)
**Good Answer:** Testimonials, case studies, data, guarantee

---

### Question 5: WHY now? (Urgency)

**Ask:**
- What's changed in the market?
- What happens if they wait?
- What opportunity are they missing?
- What's the trigger event?

**Red Flag:** No urgency (they can wait forever)
**Good Answer:** Market shift, deadline, escalating cost of inaction

## Competitive Positioning Frameworks

### Framework 1: The 2x2 Positioning Map

**Map your market on two axes:**

```
Example (Project Management Tools):

        Enterprise-Grade
              |
    Jira      |      Monday.com
              |
Complex ------+------ Simple
              |
   Asana      |      Trello
              |
        SMB-Friendly
```

**Your goal:** Find the gap (unserved quadrant)

**Questions to ask:**
- What are the two most important attributes?
- Where do competitors cluster?
- Where is the white space?
- Can you own that position?

---

### Framework 2: Category King Strategy

**Three options:**

**1. Dominate Existing Category**
- Compete head-on with incumbents
- Requires massive resources
- High risk, high reward

**2. Create New Category**
- Define a new market (you're the first)
- Own the terminology
- Category king advantage (76% of value goes to #1)

**3. Reframe Existing Category**
- Take established market
- Reposition it from new angle
- Make old solutions look obsolete

**Recommendation:** Option 2 or 3 (not option 1 unless you have $100M+)

---

### Framework 3: Enemy Positioning

**Position AGAINST something:**

**Classic Examples:**
- Avis: "We're #2, so we try harder" (vs. Hertz)
- 7-Up: "The Uncola" (vs. Coke/Pepsi)
- Apple: "Think Different" (vs. IBM)

**Why it works:**
- Clear differentiation
- Piggybacking on enemy's awareness
- Underdog appeal

**Questions:**
- Who is the incumbent/leader?
- What do they do poorly?
- What do customers hate about them?
- Can you be the opposite?

---

### Framework 4: The "Only" Statement

**Fill in the blank:**

"We're the ONLY [category] that [unique capability/approach]."

**Examples:**
- "We're the ONLY CRM built specifically for real estate investors"
- "We're the ONLY project management tool that uses AI to predict delays"
- "We're the ONLY platform that combines X + Y in one interface"

**Test:**
- Can competitors easily copy this?
- Does it matter to customers?
- Can you deliver on it?

## Positioning Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Leader

**Position:** "We're #1 in [category]"

**When to use:**
- You actually are the leader
- You have data to prove it
- Market recognizes you

**Risk:** Becomes a target for challengers

**Example:** Salesforce ("The #1 CRM")

---

### Archetype 2: The Specialist

**Position:** "We focus exclusively on [niche]"

**When to use:**
- Generalist competitors exist
- Niche is underserved
- Specific needs exist

**Risk:** Limits addressable market

**Example:** HubSpot (started as "inbound marketing only")

---

### Archetype 3: The Innovator

**Position:** "We're the first to [innovation]"

**When to use:**
- True technological advantage
- Market ready for change
- Can educate buyers

**Risk:** Education cost, early adopter only

**Example:** Tesla (first mass-market EV)

---

### Archetype 4: The Value Player

**Position:** "Same quality, lower price"

**When to use:**
- Incumbents over-priced
- Cost structure advantage
- Price-sensitive market

**Risk:** Race to the bottom, hard to escape

**Example:** Southwest Airlines

---

### Archetype 5: The Premium

**Position:** "The best [category], worth the premium"

**When to use:**
- Demonstrable quality advantage
- Target customers value quality over price
- Can justify premium

**Risk:** Smaller market, higher expectations

**Example:** Apple

## The Positioning Workshop Process

### Phase 1: Current State Assessment

**Questions:**
1. How do customers currently describe you?
2. What category do they put you in?
3. Who do they compare you to?
4. What do they think you're good/bad at?

**Method:** Customer interviews (10-15)

---

### Phase 2: Competitive Analysis

**Create this table:**

| Competitor | Target Customer | Key Benefit | Differentiator | Positioning |
|------------|----------------|-------------|----------------|-------------|
| Company A  | ...            | ...         | ...            | ...         |
| Company B  | ...            | ...         | ...            | ...         |
| You        | ...            | ...         | ...            | ...         |

**Look for:**
- Patterns (where do competitors cluster?)
- Gaps (what's not being served?)
- Overlap (who are you actually competing with?)

---

### Phase 3: Customer Pain Analysis

**Interview customers/prospects:**

**Questions:**
1. What problem were you trying to solve?
2. What were you using before?
3. Why wasn't that working?
4. What almost prevented you from buying?
5. How would you describe us to a colleague?

**Extract:**
- Pain points (in their words)
- Alternatives (what you're replacing)
- Objections (what held them back)
- Value prop (how they describe you)

---

### Phase 4: Positioning Hypothesis

**Brainstorm 3-5 positioning options:**

**Template per option:**

```
Positioning Option #1: [Name it]

Target Customer: [Who]
Pain Point: [What problem]
Category: [What type of solution]
Key Benefit: [Main value]
Differentiator: [Why us vs. them]
Proof: [Evidence]

Pros:
-
-

Cons:
-
-
```

---

### Phase 5: Testing & Validation

**How to test positioning:**

1. **Internal alignment test**
   - Can every team member explain it?
   - Does sales team embrace it?

2. **Customer comprehension test**
   - Show to 10 customers/prospects
   - Can they explain back what you do?
   - Does it resonate?

3. **Differentiation test**
   - Is it meaningfully different?
   - Can competitors easily copy?

4. **Market reality test**
   - Is there actual demand?
   - Are customers willing to pay?

5. **Sustainable advantage test**
   - Can you deliver on it?
   - Can you defend it?

## Positioning Red Flags

### ❌ Red Flag 1: "We're like [Famous Company] but for [X]"

**Problem:** You're positioning in their shadow (not creating new space)

**Better:** Create your own category

---

### ❌ Red Flag 2: "We're the best [category]"

**Problem:** Unsubstantiated, generic claim

**Better:** "We're the best [category] for [specific customer] because [specific reason]"

---

### ❌ Red Flag 3: "We do everything"

**Problem:** No clear differentiation

**Better:** Focus on ONE thing you do exceptionally well

---

### ❌ Red Flag 4: Feature-focused positioning

**Problem:** "We have AI/blockchain/cloud"

**Better:** Outcome-focused "We help you achieve [result]"

---

### ❌ Red Flag 5: No clear enemy

**Problem:** Not positioned AGAINST anything

**Better:** Identify what you're replacing or disrupting

## Messaging Hierarchy (After Positioning is Set)

### Level 1: Positioning Statement (Internal)
For [target] who [pain], we are [category] that [benefit], unlike [competitor], we [differentiator]

### Level 2: Value Proposition (External)
One sentence: "We help [target customer] achieve [outcome] through [unique approach]"

### Level 3: Tagline (Marketing)
3-7 words: Memorable, emotional, aspirational

### Level 4: Elevator Pitch (Sales)
30 seconds: Problem → Solution → Proof → CTA

### Level 5: Messaging Pillars (Content)
3-5 core themes that support positioning

## Repositioning (When to Change)

### Triggers for Repositioning:

1. **Market shifted**
   - New competitors emerged
   - Customer needs changed
   - Technology disrupted category

2. **Outgrew initial positioning**
   - Started niche, now broader
   - Upmarket or downmarket move

3. **Positioning didn't work**
   - Customers confused
   - Sales struggling
   - Wrong customer attracted

4. **Acquisition/merger**
   - Combined entities need unified position

**Warning:** Repositioning is expensive (brand equity lost). Only do when necessary.

## Positioning for Different Stages

### Pre-Launch (No Customers)
- **Focus:** Vision and category creation
- **Risk:** Hypothetical positioning (not validated)
- **Approach:** Test with early adopters

### Early Stage (1-100 Customers)
- **Focus:** Product-market fit
- **Risk:** Positioning too narrow or too broad
- **Approach:** Interview customers, refine iteratively

### Growth Stage (100-1000 Customers)
- **Focus:** Scaling and consistency
- **Risk:** Diluting positioning to grow faster
- **Approach:** Double down on what's working

### Mature Stage (1000+ Customers)
- **Focus:** Defense and evolution
- **Risk:** Disruption from new entrants
- **Approach:** Continuous innovation in positioning

## The CMO's Positioning Toolkit

When advising CEO on positioning:

### Socratic Questions:
1. "Who is this NOT for?" (Forces specificity)
2. "What do they call this problem?" (Use their language)
3. "What would they use instead of you?" (Real alternatives)
4. "Why would someone choose the competitor?" (Honest assessment)
5. "What's the one thing you want to own?" (Focus)

### Challenge Statements:
- "That positioning could apply to anyone..."
- "How is that defensible?"
- "What proof do you have?"
- "Can you explain that in 10 words?"
- "Would a customer actually say that?"

### Frameworks to Reference:
- 2x2 competitive map
- "Only" statement
- Enemy positioning
- Positioning archetypes
- Messaging hierarchy

## Positioning Success Criteria

**You have strong positioning when:**

1. ✅ Sales team can explain it in 30 seconds
2. ✅ Customers describe you the same way
3. ✅ Prospects self-qualify (right fit vs. wrong fit)
4. ✅ Competitors can't easily copy your angle
5. ✅ Pricing power increases (differentiation = less price sensitivity)
6. ✅ Marketing gets easier (clear message attracts right audience)

**You have weak positioning when:**

1. ❌ "We do lots of things for lots of people"
2. ❌ Sales team struggles to explain value
3. ❌ Customers confused about what you do
4. ❌ Price shopping (commoditized)
5. ❌ Wrong customers attracted
6. ❌ Marketing message keeps changing
